# Credit-offer experiment: design and implementation plan

> **Status: Stage 0 — plan only.** This project will use synthetic data generated by code. It does not report or imply results from a production experiment or real customer portfolio.

## Project goal

Build a reproducible, typed, tested Python portfolio project that demonstrates how to design and analyze a randomized experiment for a credit line increase (CLI). The simulated experiment asks whether offering a CLI changes card spend over 90 days while keeping serious delinquency within an explicit safety margin. Every effect in the synthetic data will be planted through documented configuration parameters, then compared with the analysis estimates.

## Experiment design

### Question and hypothesis

Among eligible simulated card accounts, does random assignment to a credit-line-increase offer change 90-day card spend, without causing an unacceptable increase in serious delinquency?

- **Primary null hypothesis:** mean 90-day spend is equal between treatment and control.
- **Primary alternative:** mean 90-day spend differs between treatment and control (two-sided test). The business hypothesis is that the CLI offer increases spend; the two-sided test avoids hiding an unexpected decrease.
- **Guardrail:** treatment must not increase the 60+ days-past-due (DPD) rate by more than the predeclared non-inferiority margin.

All accounts, outcomes, and effects in this project are synthetic. In particular, the simulation parameters are teaching assumptions, not empirically estimated credit-policy effects.

### Population, unit, and assignment

- **Population:** simulated eligible card accounts, with account attributes and a risk score generated by the simulator.
- **Randomization unit:** account. Assign each account once, 1:1, to control or treatment using a reproducible configured seed. The account-level assignment is the unit of analysis; this design assumes no interference between accounts.
- **Treatment:** assignment to a simulated CLI offer. The treatment label represents offer assignment (intent-to-treat), not proof that an account accepted or used the increase.
- **Stratification:** Stage 1 will create low, medium, and high PD risk bands. The initial assignment plan is simple 50/50 randomization; later subgroup estimates will be presented as heterogeneous-effect analyses, with their uncertainty and exploratory status made clear.

### Metrics and observation windows

- **Primary metric:** cumulative account-level spend over the 90-day post-assignment period. Analyze the difference in arithmetic means, preserving the business interpretation of average spend per randomized account.
- **Guardrail metric:** indicator of reaching 60+ DPD during the 90-day post-assignment observation window, reported as a rate and treatment-minus-control risk difference. The precise synthetic event-generation rule and observation window will be documented alongside the simulator when Stage 1 is implemented.
- **Secondary metric:** account-level balance over the 90-day post-assignment period. Treat it as secondary and do not use it to override a failed primary or guardrail criterion.
- **Pre-period covariate:** pre-period spend, measured before assignment, used for CUPED/regression adjustment. It must not be affected by treatment.

### MDE, alpha, and power

- **Minimum detectable effect (MDE):** a 5% relative change in mean 90-day spend versus the control mean. This is a planning target for the synthetic exercise, not a claim about a real portfolio or an assertion that smaller effects have no value.
- **Significance level:** two-sided `alpha = 0.05` for the primary spend test.
- **Target power:** `80%` to detect the stated MDE under the sample-size assumptions.
- **Sample-size method:** Stage 2 will use the normal-approximation two-independent-means formula as a transparent planning calculation, with baseline variance taken from the configured synthetic baseline/pilot simulation. For equal allocation, the approximate per-arm sample size is `2 * (z_(1-alpha/2) + z_power)^2 * variance / (MDE_absolute^2)`, where `MDE_absolute = 0.05 * baseline_mean`. The result will be rounded up and labeled as an approximation; the chosen variance and any CUPED variance-reduction assumption will be shown rather than hidden. A simulation-based check can be used to contextualize this approximation, not to manufacture certainty.
- **CUPED planning comparison:** report the unadjusted sample-size estimate and a separate estimate using a documented variance-reduction estimate from the pre-period/post-period relationship. Do not assume a variance reduction without measuring it in the synthetic data.
- **Guardrail margin:** use a predeclared maximum acceptable increase of **0.5 percentage points** in the 60+ DPD rate (treatment minus control). This is an explicit illustrative design choice for the synthetic project, not a regulatory or real-world credit-risk threshold. Stage 2 may additionally report the guardrail's detectable precision/power if practical; it must not silently imply that the primary spend sample size is sufficient to establish guardrail safety.

### Decision rule

Recommend **simulated “ship”** only if both conditions are met in the analysis:

1. The estimated treatment effect on 90-day spend is positive and its two-sided 95% confidence interval excludes zero; and
2. The upper bound of a one-sided 95% confidence interval for the treatment-minus-control 60+ DPD risk difference is below the illustrative `+0.5 pp` non-inferiority margin.

Otherwise recommend **do not ship based on this simulated experiment** (or “inconclusive” where the confidence interval crosses a decision boundary). A positive spend point estimate alone is not sufficient. Risk-band results will inform a cautious, explicitly simulation-only recommendation and will be interpreted with uncertainty; they will not be treated as confirmatory proof for multiple subgroups.

## Staged order of work

Each stage is a review checkpoint. Work should stop after completing a stage and summarize what changed, why, and any tests or checks performed. Do not start a later stage until the user reviews and asks to continue.

1. **Stage 0 — Plan (current):** establish the repository layout, allowed dependencies, design assumptions, metrics, power/MDE target, guardrail margin, decision rule, and stage sequence. No simulator or analysis implementation in this stage.
2. **Stage 1 — Simulation:** implement `src/simulate.py` and the shared `config.py`. Generate account-level pre-period spend, credit limit, utilization, FICO-like score, logistic PD, risk bands, seeded 50/50 assignment, correlated post-period spend, balance, and delinquency. Plant configurable spend effects by risk band and a small delinquency increase concentrated in the high-risk band. Return or expose ground-truth effects for later estimator checks. Clearly name all outputs as simulated.
3. **Stage 2 — Power analysis:** implement `src/power.py` with the stated sample-size calculation, assumptions, and an MDE-versus-sample-size plot. Compare unadjusted and CUPED-adjusted variance assumptions transparently.
4. **Stage 3 — Core analysis:** implement `src/analysis.py`: sample-ratio-mismatch chi-square check; Welch difference in means and 95% CI; CUPED estimate and variance reduction; OLS adjustment (`post_spend ~ treatment + pre_spend`) as a cross-check; 60+ DPD two-proportion analysis; and comparisons of estimates against the planted effects.
5. **Stage 4 — Pitfalls:** implement `src/pitfalls.py` to repeat correctly conducted A/A tests and demonstrate their false-positive rate, then contrast with daily repeated significance checks that stop at the first `p < 0.05`. Use fixed, separately configured seeds and explain that peeking changes the error rate.
6. **Stage 5 — Heterogeneous effects:** estimate spend and delinquency effects by PD risk band with confidence intervals. Make the CLI recommendation using the stated lift-versus-loss logic, noting subgroup uncertainty and the synthetic nature of the results.
7. **Stage 6 — Deliverables:** create the end-to-end `notebooks/walkthrough.ipynb`, complete the decision-memo `README.md`, and add pytest coverage for broken-split SRM detection, CUPED variance reduction, recovery of planted effects on a sufficiently large sample, and A/A false-positive calibration around alpha. Run and report the checks actually performed.

## Engineering and reporting principles

- Use type hints, docstrings, clear names, and small composable functions.
- Keep all simulation, analysis, power, and repetition seeds and tunable assumptions in `config.py`; avoid scattered magic numbers.
- Separate randomization assignment from outcome generation and analysis so the ground truth is testable.
- Keep confidence intervals and denominators visible, especially for delinquency and subgroup results.
- Do not describe synthetic outputs as observed customer behavior, production evidence, or real-world impact. The eventual README must prominently disclose simulated data, no network effects, and the short observation horizon, alongside the spend-versus-delinquency trade-off.
